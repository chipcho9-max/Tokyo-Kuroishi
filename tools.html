Rule of 72, in your head

Divide 72 by your return to get the years each doubling takes: at 7%, about 10 years; at 10%, about 7. A 30-year horizon at 7% is roughly three doublings — 8× your money — which is why the last decade always looks implausible next to the first.

Reading the projection honestly

Actual 30-year outcomes vary widely around any average: the same contributions could end meaningfully higher or lower depending on the sequence of returns. Treat the output as a planning anchor for your savings rate — the input you actually control — not a prediction of your account statement.
