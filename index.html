For 10, 20 and 30-year investors

Own productive assets.
Then let time do the heavy lifting.

Long Horizon is an educational field guide for people who plan to buy US stocks — from the United States, Japan, or Korea — and hold them for a decade or three. No trading signals, no price targets. Just the principles, mechanics, account structures, and taxes that matter when your holding period is measured in decades.

Who this is for

Built for holders, not traders

Everything here assumes one thing about you: when you buy, you intend to still own it in ten years — through at least two or three bear markets you cannot predict. That single assumption changes almost every decision, and it is why most financial media is useless to you.

Three home markets, one goal

Where you live changes the plumbing, not the principle

A 25-year holder in Seoul, Tokyo, and Chicago can own the very same S&P 500 — but through different accounts, different tax rules, and a different currency lens. The strategy pages treat the principle as universal and the plumbing as local.

United States

Home-market advantage

Tax-advantaged space through 401(k), IRA, and Roth accounts; low-cost index funds; long-term capital-gains rates that reward multi-year holding.

Start here

A reading path for your first hour

If you are new to investing

  1. Read Principles — especially the section on compounding and the behavior gap.
  2. Open the calculator and model your own monthly amount over 20–30 years.
  3. Read Strategy up to "Dollar-cost averaging".
  4. Follow your country's first-month checklist in Getting Started, using Accounts & Tax as the reference.

If you already invest

  1. Bookmark the Bear Market Survival page now, while you're calm — it's written for the day you'll need it.
  2. Review the common mistakes list against your current portfolio, and the Japan case study on Markets.
  3. Confirm you are using every tax-advantaged wrapper available to you in Accounts & Tax.
  4. Within ten years of spending the money? Start the Drawdown page early.
